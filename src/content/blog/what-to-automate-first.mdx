"We should be using AI for this" is the most common sentence we hear on discovery calls. It's usually followed by a list of ten things and no idea where to start.

Here is the method we use to decide, and it doesn't require buying anything.

## Step one: list the repeated work

For one week, keep a note of every task that gets done more than twice. Copying lead details into the CRM. Chasing invoices. Answering the same delivery question. Building the Monday report. Sending onboarding emails.

Be honest about who does them and how long each takes. Multiply out to a monthly figure. Most teams are surprised: it's rarely less than forty hours a month of pure repetition.

## Step two: score each task on two axes

For each task, give it a score from one to five on:

- **Time saved.** How many hours a month disappear if this runs itself?
- **Effort to build.** How many systems are involved, how messy is the data, and does it need judgement or just rules?

Plot them. The tasks with high time saved and low effort are your first automations. The ones with low time saved and high effort are not worth touching, no matter how impressive the demo.

## Step three: leave alone anything that needs taste

Automation is brilliant at rule-based work and terrible at judgement calls. Routing a lead by budget and location: automate. Deciding whether that lead is a good fit for your business: keep a human in the loop, at least at first.

The same goes for anything customer-facing where tone matters. An AI assistant that answers opening hours and delivery times is a gift. One that handles complaints unsupervised is a liability.

## Where most businesses start

Across the automations we've built, the first three are almost always:

1. **Lead intake and routing.** Enquiry arrives, gets qualified, lands in the right person's queue with context attached, within a minute.
2. **Appointment or order follow-up.** Confirmations, reminders and reschedules that run on their own.
3. **Reporting.** The weekly numbers assembled from three systems into one message, without anyone opening a spreadsheet.

None of these are glamorous. All of them pay for themselves within a couple of months.
