Every few weeks a business comes to us after a redesign that "went fine" except for one thing: organic traffic fell off a cliff. The new site looks better, loads faster and converts worse, because half the people who used to find it no longer can.

It's almost never the design's fault. It's the migration nobody owned.

## Why redesigns lose traffic

Search engines rank URLs, not websites. When a rebuild changes URLs, drops pages, rewrites titles or hides content behind JavaScript, every one of those decisions is invisible to the designer and very visible to Google.

The usual culprits:

- URLs changed without redirects, or with redirects that point to the homepage
- Pages "cleaned up" that were quietly bringing in traffic
- Title tags and meta descriptions reset to defaults
- Internal links that now point to 404s
- Structured data and canonical tags left behind on the old platform

## The checklist we run on every rebuild

1. **Crawl the old site before touching anything.** Every URL, its title, its inbound links and its organic traffic over the last twelve months.
2. **Rank URLs by revenue, not by page count.** The twenty pages that bring in most of the traffic get individual attention; the long tail gets rule-based redirects.
3. **Map every URL to a destination.** One-to-one where the page still exists, to the closest equivalent where it doesn't. Never to the homepage.
4. **Carry over metadata and structured data.** Titles, descriptions, canonicals, schema. Then improve them, but start from what worked.
5. **Validate analytics on staging.** If conversion tracking isn't firing before launch, you won't know what the redesign did.
6. **Launch in phases where the site is large.** Templates first, then sections, watching Search Console between each.
7. **Keep redirects live for at least a year.** Old links live in emails, PDFs and other people's websites long after you've forgotten them.

> A redesign should be invisible to Google and obvious to customers. If it's the other way round, something went wrong in planning, not in design.

## What "no SEO loss" actually looks like

Rankings typically wobble for two to four weeks after a migration while search engines re-crawl. What you should not see is a sustained drop. On well-run replatforms we usually see traffic flat through the wobble and then rising, because the new site is faster and better structured.
